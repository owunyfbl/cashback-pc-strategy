## How the money actually moves

ShopBack is a cashback platform, founded in Singapore in 2014 and licensed there as a Major Payment Institution since June 2025. The mechanic is simple: you start your shopping session on ShopBack, click through to the retailer, and the retailer pays ShopBack an affiliate commission for the referral. ShopBack passes a slice of that commission back to you.

Three consequences fall out of that, and all three matter when you're buying a PC:

- **The price at checkout doesn't change.** You pay what everyone else pays. Cashback arrives afterwards.
- **It stacks with sale pricing**, because it comes out of the retailer's marketing budget, not the product price.
- **It does not stack with other affiliate coupon codes**, and that's where most gaming PC cashback dies. More on that below.

The upside of the after-the-fact model: a 9.9 or Black Friday price drop plus a cashback rate doesn't involve choosing one over the other. The downside: you're waiting weeks for the money, and the rate you see today may not be the rate you checked last month. Rates on the retail side move constantly.

## The actual math on a big-ticket build

Illustrative numbers, since your cart is your cart. Say you're spending $1,500 on a prebuilt, and the store's cashback rate is 1%.

1% of $1,500 is $15. That arrives in roughly 85 to 120 days, depending on the merchant, and it's calculated on the purchase price *excluding* taxes, shipping and fees. So if you're in a state with sales tax and you're also paying $40 for delivery, your cashback is based on the pre-tax, pre-shipping figure.

Fifteen dollars on a $1,500 purchase is not a reason to choose one store over another. It's a reason to not leave it on the table when you were buying from that store anyway.

Where cashback sizing gets genuinely interesting is at the high end: a 3% rate on a $2,500 build is $75, and upsized promotional rates during major sale events can push that meaningfully higher. That's the scenario where it's worth twenty minutes of checking rates before you buy. It's also the scenario where the cap and exclusion rules start mattering most, because that's the order size where a rejected claim hurts.

## What voids your cashback on a PC order

This is the section that saves you money, and it's the one most cashback explainers skip.

1. **Using a coupon code from anywhere other than ShopBack.** Lenovo's terms explicitly exclude purchases made with codes imported from other sites, and Newegg excludes codes with its standard promotional prefixes. You can lose the entire cashback to save 5% with an outside code — which is often still a good trade, but do the arithmetic rather than assuming they stack.
2. **Paying with a gift card or store credit.** Both Lenovo and Newegg exclude gift card and voucher purchases. If part of your order is paid with store credit, that portion typically isn't eligible.
3. **Running an ad blocker or VPN.** Ad blockers strip the affiliate tracking cookie. VPNs and third-party browser extensions can do the same. Turn them off for the session, or use the mobile app.
4. **Not starting from ShopBack.** Every transaction needs its own click-through. Navigating directly to a store you've used through ShopBack before won't track. This is the single most common reason cashback "just doesn't show up".
5. **Switching browsers or tabs, or jumping to the retailer's app.** The tracking tag lives in the browser session that created it. Click through on desktop, finish on desktop.
6. **Leaving items in your cart.** Lenovo's terms require you to clear your cart before using the click-through, and items already sitting in the cart won't track.
7. **Declining cookies.** Merchants can only confirm a transaction if you accept the cookies on their pages.
8. **Returning or exchanging the order.** Refunds, cancellations and exchanges aren't eligible, and partial returns get prorated down.

Note where the risk concentrates for gaming PCs specifically: exclusion lists are usually written around consoles, game software and gift cards, while PC components and prebuilt desktops generally sit in the eligible categories — but "generally" is doing work in that sentence, and the exclusion list on the merchant page is the only version that counts. On a configurable system where you're also adding a monitor, keyboard and headset, check each category separately, because the rates aren't uniform across a store.

## When cashback is the wrong tool

Three cases where I'd tell you to skip it:

**You need the money off now.** Cashback on a Lenovo order confirms in around 85 days; Newegg takes 90; Woot! takes 120. If the build is a stretch this month, a cashback payout in three months isn't a discount, it's a delayed rebate. A cheaper configuration is the better move.

**You're buying from a small boutique builder.** The network skews toward large retailers and manufacturer stores. Check that the builder has a ShopBack page before you assume anything — if they're not listed, there's nothing to route.

**Your store's rate is at the bottom of the range and your order is small.** 0.8% on a $400 budget build is $3.20, arriving in 90 days. Do it if it's zero effort. Don't restructure your purchase around it.

## Pre-checkout checklist

Run this in order, because the sequence is what makes the tracking hold:

1. Check the live rate on the merchant's ShopBack page — not a cached rate from a deals forum.
2. Read the exclusion list for the specific categories in your cart.
3. Empty your existing cart at the retailer, then close the store's app.
4. Disable ad blockers and VPN.
5. Start from ShopBack, click through, and complete the purchase in that same browser session.
6. Accept cookies on the retailer's site.
7. Skip outside coupon codes, or accept that you're trading the cashback for them.
8. Keep the order number and confirmation email. If nothing tracks within a couple of days, you'll need them.

## FAQ

**Does cashback work on custom-configured gaming PCs?**
It depends on the store's exclusion list rather than on whether the system is prebuilt or configured. Category-level exclusions vary inside a single store, so check the specific category your configuration falls under.

**Can I use cashback and a student discount?**
Lenovo's terms exclude purchases made through its Education Store, and separately exclude purchases using discount types that lower the advertised price, including student education codes. Pick one.

**What if my cashback doesn't appear?**
Merchant pages list the tracking window — two days for Newegg, Lenovo and Woot!. If nothing shows in that window, raise it with the order number in hand. Cashback that tracks at a different rate initially is normal; it gets adjusted when the transaction is confirmed.

**Is the "up to X%" headline the rate I'll get?**
Usually not. Headline percentages generally apply to a specific category or a specific campaign window, and the category rate for PC hardware is often lower than the banner suggests. The number on the merchant page for your category is the one that pays.
